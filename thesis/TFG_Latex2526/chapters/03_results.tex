\chapter{Results}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter reports the numerical and qualitative outcomes of the three-tier validation framework defined in \Cref{sec:methodology-validation}. Metrics, acceptance bands, and scope exclusions are not repeated here; each section references the corresponding methodology subsection.

\section{Pipeline Execution: ASOCA vs.\ MACS-18}
\label{sec:results-pipeline}

The full 40-patient cohort was processed end-to-end on both ASOCA and MACS-18 masks (\Cref{subsec:validation-asoca,subsec:validation-macs}). Evaluation was limited to operational reliability, geometric extraction quality, and runtime; clinical stenosis accuracy was excluded (\Cref{sec:methodology-validation}).

\subsection{Execution and Endpoint Topology}
\label{subsec:results-execution-endpoints}

Both cohorts achieved an Execution Success Rate (ESR) of 100\%: all 40 normal and 40 diseased cases completed Blocks~1--4 without fatal error.

\subsection{Runtime}
\label{subsec:results-runtime}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{{Figures/1 Pipeline Execution ASOCA vs. MACS-18/Figure 5. Runtimes ASPCA vs. MACS18.png}}
  \caption{Per-block and total pipeline runtimes for ASOCA and MACS-18 cohorts.}
  \label{fig:runtimes-comparison}
\end{figure}

Mean total runtime was 346.9\,s (5.78\,min) on ASOCA and 576.2\,s (9.60\,min) on MACS-18. Both cohort means fall within the good band ($<$900\,s) of \Cref{tab:validation-real-thresholds}. These values reflect automated batch processing time only and are not directly comparable to full manual CCTA review, as the pipeline is intended to support rather than replace expert visual assessment (\Cref{subsec:validation-real-metrics}). Block~2 (geometric quantification) dominated runtime on both cohorts, with a higher mean on MACS-18 (357.7\,s vs.\ 164.6\,s on ASOCA), consistent with denser geometry and more cross-section evaluations. Mean Block~1, Block~3, and Block~4 runtimes were 145.8\,s, 35.2\,s, and 0.16\,s (ASOCA) versus 159.8\,s, 57.9\,s, and 0.02\,s (MACS-18), respectively.

Diagnostic confidence (Q9) was neutral for one participant and positive for two, consistent with a decision-support rather than autonomous diagnostic role. Qualitative feedback from the evaluation session is discussed in \Cref{sec:discussion-future}.
