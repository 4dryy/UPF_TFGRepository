\section{Pipeline Execution: ASOCA vs.\ MACS-18}
\label{sec:results-pipeline}

\Cref{fig:centerlines-surfaces-comparison} introduces the paired ASOCA versus MACS-18 comparison using patient Normal\_8, a representative normal case from the shared 40-patient cohort. The figure contrasts Block~1 centerline extraction, endpoint detection, ostium identification, and surface mesh generation when only the input lumen mask differs between datasets.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{{Figures/1 Pipeline Execution ASOCA vs. MACS-18/Figure 1 and 2 Merged.pdf}}
  \caption{Block~1 outputs for Normal\_8 in four side-by-side panels. Left pair (ASOCA, then MACS-18): centerline paths with red ostium points and blue branch endpoints. Right pair (ASOCA, then MACS-18): smoothed lumen surfaces with light-red LCA and light-blue RCA.}
  \label{fig:centerlines-surfaces-comparison}
\end{figure}

As stated in \Cref{subsec:datasets-asoca-macs}, MACS-18 provides more anatomically detailed lumen masks than ASOCA. For the same patient (e.g., Normal\_8), this difference can be observed in both the centerline and surface views, where MACS-18 yields sharper branch definition.

On ASOCA masks, every sample completed Blocks~1--4 without fatal error (ESR = 100\%, \emph{good}). The cohort averaged 17.1 detected endpoints per patient, with 89.77\% correctly identified (EISR, \emph{good}). Ostium placement reached 85.0\% success (OISR, \emph{good}), and centerline extraction succeeded in 83.19\% of attempted paths (CESR, \emph{neutral}). Mean total runtime was 346.9\,s (\emph{good}).

On MACS-18 masks, every sample completed Blocks~1--4 without fatal error (ESR = 100\%, \emph{good}). Mean endpoints per patient increased to 27.7, consistent with the richer distal branching in the refined masks. Ostium success was 73.75\% (OISR, \emph{poor}), and centerline extraction improved to 95.62\% (CESR, \emph{good}). Mean total runtime was 576.2\,s (\emph{neutral}).

\section{Geometric and Algorithmic Accuracy via Synthetic Data}
\label{sec:results-synthetic}

Block~2 quantification was verified on the two synthetic vessel models defined in \Cref{subsec:validation-synthetic}. The analytical ground truth for lumen area and \%AS specified in that section isolates algorithmic error from the anatomical variability of the real cohorts in \Cref{sec:results-pipeline}. Both models were processed through the full pipeline; \Cref{fig:synthetic-tubes} shows the resulting tube reconstructions with extracted centerlines.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.88\linewidth]{{Figures/2 Synthetic Samples/Figure 6. Synthetic Tubes with Centerline.pdf}}
  \caption{Extracted centerlines on the synthetic validation models. Left: \texttt{synthetic\_1} (healthy, uniform radius). Right: \texttt{synthetic\_2} (diseased, mid-vessel stenosis).}
  \label{fig:synthetic-tubes}
\end{figure}

As \Cref{tab:synthetic-validation-outcomes} below shows, pipeline execution on both synthetic models yields absolute errors that all fall within the \emph{good} acceptance bands of \Cref{tab:validation-synthetic-thresholds}. For each metric, the analytical ground truth defined in \Cref{subsec:validation-synthetic} is compared against the computed Block~2 output; the largest deviations occur on \texttt{synthetic\_2}, with a maximum \%AS error of 1.31\% and a maximum area error of 4.50\,mm$^2$. Additional visualizations and information about these results, including longitudinal area and \%AS profiles, are provided in \Cref{app:synthetic-profiles}.

\section{Coronary Artery Dashboard Visualization Output}
